# Business front desk: implementation note

**Choices.** FastAPI serves the API and a plain HTML/JavaScript interface using Bootstrap controls. SQLite keeps local startup simple and preserves customers, appointments, quotes, conversations, rules, and schedules. Anthropic is the default AI provider; the official Anthropic SDK and LiteLLM provide a configurable adapter for other providers. Pydantic validates structured decisions. The AI interprets requests, while Python checks availability and commits actions. Booking transactions recheck technician and customer overlap before saving. APScheduler handles recurrence, and stored delivery identifiers prevent duplicate reminders and reports. Docker Compose provides one-command startup.

**Assumptions.** Each instance serves one business through one server process. Business name, services, timezone, hours, visit duration, and technicians are configurable. Technicians share working hours and can perform every configured service. Returning customers are identified through the demo selector. A booking needs a service request, customer name, and address; gate codes are optional. Messaging and calendars are simulated. “Waiting on a quote” means the business still owes an estimate. Reports support daily or single-weekday schedules at a local time. Existing appointments remain scheduled when an owner closes a day to new bookings.

**What was left out.** Login, real SMS/email delivery, external calendar synchronization, route optimization, technician-specific skills and shifts, cancellation/rescheduling, and an itemized pricing engine are outside this version. Quote records track requests and status. Owner-defined automation is limited to the morning rundown, quotes owed, and open handoffs. Arbitrary workflows and monthly or multi-weekday report schedules are deferred.

**With another week.** I would add cancellation and rescheduling, technician-specific availability, and editable business settings in the interface. I would expand live conversation checks for ambiguous dates, changed requests, interrupted bookings, and provider failures. I would also verify the complete walkthrough against a second business configuration to identify remaining assumptions tied to the plumbing demo.

**At thousands of businesses.** I would move to PostgreSQL with tenant-scoped records and settings, authenticated business accounts, and database-enforced booking constraints. Scheduled work would move to a durable queue and separate workers, with retries and per-business workload limits. Real messaging would use an outbox, provider delivery tracking, and duplicate-send prevention. Provider credentials, timezones, and schedules would belong to each tenant. Monitoring would cover failed deliveries, overdue jobs, booking conflicts, and AI cost and latency.

**AI-tool use and revisions.** AI coding tools assisted with implementation, documentation, and workflow checks. The initial interface and copy were rewritten into literal workflow labels and a linked requirements index. The initial provider choice was replaced with an Anthropic-default adapter. Live testing exposed missing structured fields and a generic handoff summary; the implementation was revised to require all response fields and retain the original customer request when a summary is absent.

**Verification.** Sixteen automated checks passed, covering booking conflicts, changed availability, missing details, handoff/resume, quote follow-up, report recurrence, persistence, duplicate-delivery prevention, and provider validation. A live Claude walkthrough with fictional records verified booking, saved addresses and gate codes, owner intervention, Sunday closure, and Friday report delivery. Docker startup and health, persisted records, documentation routes, and desktop and narrow-screen layouts were also checked. The repeatable walkthrough is documented in README.md.
